    Chapter 02 · The basics

    Automation, custom AI assistants and AI agents

    Many office workers spend hours a day as a copy-paste bridge between two systems. Automation builds that bridge in software. AI makes it smart enough to read messy emails and PDFs. Agents let software finish a whole multi-step job and ask a person only when something is unusual.

    The four levels

    1 · Rule automation

    IF this happens, THEN do that. No AI.

    New form → sheet → email team

    Build: $500–$1,500

    2 · AI automation

    A fixed workflow with one AI step that reads, writes or sorts.

    Invoice email → AI reads PDF → QuickBooks

    Build: $1,500–$5,000

    3 · Custom AI assistant

    Answers questions from the company's own files and systems.

    "What's our refund policy for order 1042?" in Slack

    Build: $3,000–$8,000

    4 · AI agent

    Gets a goal and tools; decides its steps in a loop; humans approve exceptions.

    "Qualify this lead and book a meeting"

    Build: $5,000–$15,000

    Price ranges: Vantaige 2026 AI automation rate card (market-observed agency ranges, not guarantees).

    How an AI agent works

    1 · Goal

    "Process today's invoices"

    2 · Plan

    The AI brain (GPT, Claude) decides the next step

    3 · Use a tool

    Read email, query the ERP, write a sheet

    4 · Check

    Did it work? Any exception?

    5 · Repeat / finish

    Loop until done; hand edge cases to a person

    Guardrails

    Limits, approvals, logs. Never pays money alone

    In one line: an automation follows a fixed path you drew; an agent chooses its own path toward a goal, inside limits you set.

    What a custom AI assistant is

    A custom AI assistant is like ChatGPT, but it knows your company. It is connected to the company's documents (SOPs, policies, prices, contracts) and live systems (CRM, TMS, accounting), and answers in Slack, Teams, email or on the website, with sources. The pattern is called RAG (retrieval-augmented generation): the AI looks things up before answering.

    Plain ChatGPTCustom AI assistant
    Knows the internet, not your companyKnows your SOPs, prices, policies, clients and history
    Staff copy-paste data into itConnected directly to CRM, TMS, accounting, shared drives
    Everyone sees the same thingRespects permissions: a dispatcher can't see payroll
    No company logEvery question and answer logged and reviewed
    Answers can drift from policyGrounded in approved documents, with sources
    Lives in a browser tabLives where work happens: Slack, Teams, email, website, phone

    How agents handle repeated tasks

    RhythmTriggered byExamples
    InstantNew email, form, file, order or webhookInvoice arrives → bill drafted; new lead → reply in 2 minutes
    Every few minutesChecking a system for changesNew loads on a board; TMS status changes
    DailySchedule (e.g. 7 am)Bank reconciliation; overdue invoice reminders; check-call summaries
    Weekly / monthlySchedule + calendarMonday KPI report; month-end checklist; variance commentary
    On requestA person asks in Slack/Teams"Summarise this contract"; "Draft the client update"

    Agents work through a queue: each invoice, lead or load is processed the same way, retried if a system is down, and sent to a person when confidence is low.

    Why a custom agent instead of off-the-shelf software

    Their process is unique

    Off-the-shelf tools cover about 60%. The last 40%, with its exceptions and odd systems, is where the hours are.

    Their systems don't talk

    TMS, QuickBooks, Gmail, Excel and a portal: an agent connects all of them.

    Cheaper than a hire

    A $6,000 build plus $1,200 a month is far less than a US hire, and it runs 24/7.

    Report

    The finance analysis agent: from Excel to answers

    Ask in plain English

    • "Why is gross margin down 3 points this month?"
    • "Which customers are over 60 days late?"
    • "What does cash look like in 6 weeks if payroll rises 10%?"
    • "Draft this month's variance commentary."

    How it's built

    • Data: QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite, Stripe, bank feeds
    • Automation: n8n scheduled pulls, checks, alerts
    • Calculation: Python or SQL (AI explains, code calculates)
    • Delivery: Slack/Teams assistant, email summary, Power BI or Looker Studio
    Finance guardrails (non-negotiable)The agent never sends payments or posts final journal entries without approval. Read-only first; drafts only. Segregation of duties. Full audit trail. Calculations by code, not the AI model. Follow the client's SOX controls where they apply.

      Security standard

      • MFA everywhere; company laptops only
      • Credentials in n8n credentials or a vault, never in code or chat
      • Read-only until write is needed; separate test and live
      • Client owns all accounts and AI keys
      • Logs for every run; minimal personal data in prompts
      • Business/API tiers of AI providers (check data-use terms)

      Main risks

      • AI mistakes: real-sample tests, thresholds, approvals, shadow mode
      • System changes: error alerts and care plan
      • Liability: liability cap, client approves outputs, E&O
      • Data breach: security standard, cyber insurance
      • Commoditisation: industry-specific multi-system builds
      • Scope creep: fixed scope and change requests
